// kicad · rp2040 · pcb design

Custom Mechanical
Macro Keypad

Writing firmware · Schematic to fabrication in KiCad

The idea

A 3×3 mechanical macro keypad, designed entirely from scratch — schematic, PCB layout, and fabrication — instead of starting from a reference board.

Designing it

Built around an RP2040: native USB support, and a PIO peripheral well-suited to matrix scanning. USB-C for power and data. Nine mechanical switches in a 3×3 matrix, each with its own diode to prevent ghosting and enable full N-key rollover. Supporting components (crystal oscillator, decoupling caps, USBLC6 ESD protection) placed by following the RP2040's datasheet and reference design.

From layout to real boards

Routed a 2-layer PCB, ran ERC/DRC to catch unconnected nets and clearance violations, checked every footprint against its datasheet land pattern, then sent Gerber and drill files to JLCPCB for fabrication.

Where it stands

Second board attempt — the first had shorting power rails I couldn't fully isolate even with continuity checks and component removal, so I started over on a fresh board. Hand-soldered this one myself (mostly tiny 0603 parts), including clearing a couple of solder bridges off the USB-C connector's VBUS/GND pads. Assembly is done and clean. What's left is the firmware: key-matrix scanning as a real USB HID device.
